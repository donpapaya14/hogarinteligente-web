## Why Incandescent Bulbs are Energy Guzzlers

Incandescent bulbs waste **90% of their energy as heat**, with only 10% converted to light, according to the [U.S. Department of Energy (2019)](https://www.energy.gov). This inefficiency makes them costly over time. For example, a 60W incandescent bulb running 5 hours daily consumes **110 kWh annually**, while an equivalent 9W LED uses just **16 kWh**—saving **$12 per bulb yearly**.

## LED Bulb Efficiency: A Closer Look

LED bulbs last **25,000 hours on average—25x longer than incandescent bulbs** ([Energy Star, 2022](https://www.energystar.gov)). They also deliver **80-100 lumens per watt**, compared to incandescent’s 16 lumens. A single LED replacement can save **$55 in electricity costs over its lifespan**.

| Metric | Incandescent | LED |
|--------|--------------|-----|

## Real-World Examples of Energy Savings

A [National Association of Home Builders (2018)](https://www.nahb.org) survey found households switching to LEDs reduced lighting costs by **$225 annually**. In a case study, a Texas family cut their electricity bill by **18%** after replacing 30 incandescent bulbs.

## The Payback Period: When to Expect Savings

LED bulbs typically pay for themselves in **under 1 year**. Here’s the math:
1. **Cost**: $5 per LED bulb (vs. $1 for incandescent)
2. **Savings**: $12/year in electricity
3. **Break-even**: 5 months ([U.S. DOE, 2020](https://www.energy.gov))

LED bulbs save **$10-$12 annually per bulb** compared to incandescent, per the [U.S. DOE](https://www.energy.gov). A household with 20 bulbs can save **$200+ yearly**.

Most LEDs fit standard sockets. For enclosed fixtures, check for "Enclosed Rated" labels ([Energy Star](https://www.energystar.gov)).
