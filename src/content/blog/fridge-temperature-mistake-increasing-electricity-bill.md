## Introduction to Refrigerator Temperature Setting Mistake
The average household sets their fridge 2°F colder than needed, at 37°F instead of the recommended 39°F, resulting in an unnecessary $30+ increase in their annual electricity bill, according to the [ENERGY STAR 2023 Appliance Standards](https://www.energystar.gov/).

## The 2°F Error Most People Make
This mistake is common due to misconceptions about food safety and the optimal refrigerator temperature. In reality, 39°F is equally safe for food preservation but uses **5-8% less energy** than 37°F.

| Temperature (°F) | Energy Consumption (%) |
| --- | --- |
| 37 | 100% |
| 39 | 92-95% |

## Why Your Milk Doesn't Need Arctic Temps
According to the [FDA Food Code 2022](https://www.fda.gov/), 39°F is sufficient for preserving dairy products and other perishables. This temperature setting reduces energy waste without compromising food safety.

## The Hidden Impact of Overcrowding
Overpacking your fridge can block airflow, forcing the compressor to work **30% harder** and increasing energy consumption. For example, if your fridge normally consumes 1.5 kWh/day, overcrowding can increase this to 1.95 kWh/day.

| Fridge Organization | Energy Consumption (kWh/day) |
| --- | --- |
| Normal | 1.5 |
| Overcrowded | 1.95 |

## The 5-Minute Temperature Check Hack
To verify your fridge's actual temperature, follow these steps:
1. Place a glass of water in the fridge.
2. Wait 30 minutes for the water to reach the fridge's temperature.
3. Use a thermometer to measure the water's temperature.
This method is more accurate than relying on the built-in thermostat.
