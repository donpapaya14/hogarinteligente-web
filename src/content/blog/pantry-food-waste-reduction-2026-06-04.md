## Understanding Food Waste Statistics
The average American household throws away **40% of the food they purchase**, resulting in an estimated **219 pounds of food waste per person per year**, according to the [United States Environmental Protection Agency (2022)](https://www.epa.gov). The most commonly wasted foods include **fruits and vegetables**, with **30-40%** of these items being discarded. This not only affects the environment but also has significant economic implications, with the average household losing around **$1,500 per year** due to food waste.

## Pantry Purge and Categorization
To tackle food waste, start by clearing out your pantry. Here's a step-by-step guide:
1. **Remove all items** from your pantry and sort them into categories (e.g., grains, canned goods, baking supplies).
2. **Check expiration dates** and dispose of any expired or spoiled items.
3. **Categorize and group similar items** together, making it easier to see what you have and what you need. The [National Foundation for Credit Counseling (2020)](https://www.nfcc.org) suggests implementing a 'first-in, first-out' policy to ensure older items are consumed before they expire.

## Optimal Food Storage Methods
Proper food storage is crucial for maintaining freshness and reducing waste. Here are some practical tips:
- **Store grains** in airtight containers to keep them fresh for up to **6 months**.
- **Keep canned goods** in a cool, dry place, and use them within **5-10 years** of the expiration date.
- **Label and date** all stored items so you can easily keep track of what you have and how long it's been stored. The [Academy of Nutrition and Dietetics (2021)](https://www.andjrnl.org) recommends using clear, airtight containers for storing food to maintain freshness and visibility.

## Meal Planning and Grocery List Strategies
Planning your meals around what's already in your pantry can significantly reduce food waste. Here's how:
1. **Take stock** of what you already have in your pantry, fridge, and freezer.
2. **Plan meals** around these items, making sure to include a variety of food groups.
3. **Make a grocery list** based on what you need for your planned meals, avoiding impulse buys. The [American Heart Association (2019)](https://www.heart.org) suggests planning your meals for the week, including breakfast, lunch, dinner, and snacks, to ensure you're using up all the food you purchase.

## Creative Uses for Common Pantry Items
Don't let common pantry items go to waste. Here are some creative uses:
- **Bread heels** can be used to make croutons or bread pudding.
- **Vegetable scraps** can be used to make broth or stock.
- **Leftover grains** can be used to make fried rice or as a base for salads. The [United States Department of Agriculture (2020)](https://www.usda.gov) provides a wealth of information on creative ways to use up food scraps and reduce waste.

## Maintaining the Pantry System
To keep your pantry organized and clutter-free, follow these tips:
1. **Schedule regular clean-outs** every **3-6 months** to get rid of expired or unused items.
2. **Implement a maintenance routine** that includes checking expiration dates and restocking shelves. The [National Association of Professional Organizers (2022)](https://www.napo.net) recommends setting aside time each week to maintain your pantry and prevent clutter from building up.
