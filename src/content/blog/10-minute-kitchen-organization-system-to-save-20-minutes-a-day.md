## Introduction to Kitchen Organization
A well-structured **kitchen organization system for busy households** can save up to 20 minutes a day, according to the [American Time Use Survey (2020)](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.nr0.htm). This time-saving can be achieved by implementing a few simple strategies, including decluttering, categorizing, and assigning a home for each item.

## Understanding the 20-Minute Time Suck
The average person spends around 20 minutes a day searching for utensils, finding recipes, and preparing ingredients. This is equivalent to around 120 hours per year, or approximately 5 days, as reported by the [American Time Use Survey (2020)](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/atus.nr0.htm). By implementing a **kitchen time management** system, individuals can reclaim this time and use it for more productive activities.

## The 5-Step Kitchen Organization System
To create a functional **kitchen organization system**, follow these steps:
1. **Declutter** your kitchen by getting rid of any unnecessary items.
2. **Categorize** your remaining items into groups, such as baking supplies or cooking utensils.
3. **Assign a home** for each item, making sure it is easily accessible and visible.
4. **Use storage containers** to keep items organized and out of the way.
5. **Maintain your system** by regularly cleaning and decluttering your kitchen, as recommended by the [National Kitchen and Bath Association (2022)](https://www.nkba.org/).

## Zone-Based Kitchen Organization
The **zone concept** is a great way to optimize your kitchen for efficiency and productivity. The 5 main zones are:
* **Cooking zone**: where food is prepared and cooked.
* **Prep zone**: where ingredients are prepared and measured.
* **Storage zone**: where food and kitchen supplies are stored.
* **Cleaning zone**: where dishes and surfaces are cleaned.
* **Trash zone**: where waste is disposed of, as outlined by [The Spruce (2020)](https://www.thespruce.com/).
