## Why rinsing doesn't clean your toothbrush

Rinsing your toothbrush with water may not be enough to eliminate all germs. According to a University of Manchester study (2019), rinsing can leave behind up to 100 million bacteria, including E. coli from splashed toilet water. This is concerning, especially if you share your toothbrush or have a weakened immune system.

## How UV-C light destroys microorganisms

UV-C light, with a wavelength of 285nm, is a proven method for killing bacteria, viruses, and mold. This technology is used in hospital equipment to disinfect surfaces and is effective against a wide range of microorganisms. The FDA has classified UV-C light as a medical device, citing its effectiveness in reducing microbial contamination (2021).

Independent testing by NSF International Certification (2022) has shown that a UV toothbrush sanitizer can reduce germs by 99.9% in just 7 minutes. Many users may not be aware of the built-in drying mode in their UV toothbrush sanitizer. This feature is crucial for preventing mold growth, especially on electric toothbrush heads. According to the American Dental Association guidelines, it's essential to dry your toothbrush thoroughly after use to prevent bacterial growth.
