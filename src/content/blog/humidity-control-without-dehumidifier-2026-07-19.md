## Understanding Humidity and Its Impact
According to the American Society of Heating, Refrigerating and Air-Conditioning Engineers (ASHRAE) 2017, **absolute humidity** refers to the mass of water vapor per unit volume of air, while **relative humidity** is the ratio of the amount of water vapor in the air to the maximum amount of water vapor the air can hold at a given temperature. High humidity levels can lead to mold growth, appliance lifespan reduction, and increased energy consumption.

**Mold growth**: A study by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) 2015 found that mold growth can occur within 24-48 hours in environments with relative humidity above 60%. In contrast, a study by the University of Sydney (2021, BMJ) found that a relative humidity of 40% or lower significantly reduced mold growth.

**Appliance lifespan**: High humidity levels can reduce the lifespan of appliances by causing corrosion and rust. A study by the International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials (IAPMO) 2020 found that a relative humidity of 50% or higher can reduce the lifespan of appliances by up to 50%.

**Energy consumption**: High humidity levels can increase energy consumption by requiring more energy to cool and dehumidify the air. A study by the Energy Information Administration (EIA) 2019 found that a relative humidity of 60% or higher can increase energy consumption by up to 20%.

## Using Desiccants for Humidity Control
Desiccants are materials that can absorb and release moisture from the air. There are several types of desiccants, including silica gel, activated alumina, and calcium chloride. A study by the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) 2015 found that silica gel is the most effective desiccant for humidity control, with a moisture absorption capacity of up to 40%.
