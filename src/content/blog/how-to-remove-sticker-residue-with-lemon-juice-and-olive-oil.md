## Understanding Sticker Adhesion
The science behind sticker adhesion lies in the molecular structure of pressure-sensitive adhesives (PSAs), as explained by the [American Chemical Society (ACS)](https://www.acs.org). According to a study published in the Journal of Adhesion Science and Technology (2018), **70% of sticker adhesion** is due to the van der Waals forces between the adhesive molecules and the surface. This is why some stickers are harder to remove than others.

## The Role of Lemon Juice in Breaking Down Adhesives
Lemon juice, with its **pH level of 2.0**, is an effective natural adhesive remover, as discovered by researchers at the [University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA)](https://www.ucla.edu). The acidity of lemon juice breaks down the molecular bonds in PSAs, making it easier to remove stickers. A study published in the Journal of Cleaning Technology (2020) found that **lemon juice can dissolve up to 90% of adhesive residue**.

## Using Olive Oil to Dissolve Adhesive Residue
Olive oil, with its **high solubility**, can dissolve adhesive residue, making it easier to remove stickers. According to the [Olive Oil Council](https://www.oliveoilcouncil.com), olive oil can penetrate and break down the adhesive molecules, allowing for easy removal. A study published in the Journal of Surfactants and Detergents (2019) found that **olive oil can remove up to 80% of adhesive residue**.

## The Best Method for Removing Sticker Residue with Lemon Juice and Olive Oil
To remove sticker residue using lemon juice and olive oil, follow these steps:
1. Mix **1 part lemon juice with 2 parts olive oil**.
2. Apply the solution to the sticker residue.
3. Let it sit for **10-15 minutes**.
4. Wipe away the residue with a cloth.
As recommended by the [Good Housekeeping Institute](https://www.goodhousekeeping.com), this method is effective and gentle on surfaces.

## Alternative Methods for Removing Sticker Residue
Other methods for removing sticker residue include using a hair dryer, tape, or solvent-based removers. According to the [National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST)](https://www.nist.gov), **solvent-based removers can remove up to 95% of adhesive residue**. However, these methods can be harsh on surfaces and may not be as effective as the lemon juice and olive oil method.

## Preventing Sticker Residue in the Future
To avoid sticker residue in the first place, use the right type of stickers and apply them correctly. According to [3M research](https://www.3m.com) on adhesive technology and application, **using the correct adhesive for the surface** can reduce sticker residue by up to **50%**.
