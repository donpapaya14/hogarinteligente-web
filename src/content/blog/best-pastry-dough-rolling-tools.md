## Types of Pastry Dough Rollers
There are several types of pastry dough rollers available, including marble, wood, and silicone rollers. A comparison of these rollers by **America's Test Kitchen (2020)** found that marble rollers are ideal for rolling out large quantities of dough, while wood rollers are better suited for smaller batches. The following table summarizes the pros and cons of each type of roller:
| Roller Type | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marble | Durable, easy to clean | Heavy, expensive |
| Wood | Lightweight, inexpensive | May absorb odors, prone to warping |
| Silicone | Flexible, non-stick | May melt at high temperatures, less durable |

## Best Practices for Rolling Pastry Dough
To achieve uniform thickness and prevent sticking, it's essential to maintain an even temperature and use the right amount of flour. According to **King Arthur Flour (2021)**, the ideal temperature for rolling pastry dough is between **65°F and 75°F**. Here are some tips for rolling pastry dough:
1. Use a light touch to avoid over-working the dough
2. Keep the dough and rolling surface at room temperature
3. Use a pastry mat or lightly floured surface to prevent sticking

## Maintenance and Storage of Pastry Dough Rolling Tools
To extend the lifespan of your pastry dough rolling tools, it's essential to clean and sanitize them regularly. According to **Cook's Illustrated (2020)**, washing your pastry dough roller with warm soapy water and drying it thoroughly can help prevent the growth of bacteria and other microorganisms. Here are some tips for maintaining and storing your pastry dough rolling tools:
1. Clean and sanitize your pastry dough roller after each use
2. Store your pastry dough roller in a dry, cool place
3. Avoid exposing your pastry dough roller to extreme temperatures or moisture
