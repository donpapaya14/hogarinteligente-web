## Best thermostat settings for energy savings in winter

Winter is here, and with it comes the challenge of keeping your home warm while minimizing energy costs. The ideal thermostat settings for energy savings in winter are a delicate balance between comfort and efficiency. According to the U.S. Department of Energy (2022), the optimal daytime temperature for energy savings is **68°F (20°C)**.

### Ideal daytime temperature for energy savings

The U.S. Department of Energy found that for every degree of temperature change above 68°F, heating costs increase by 3-5% (2022). For example, if you set your thermostat to 72°F, you can expect to pay 6-10% more for heating than if you set it to 68°F. This may not seem like a lot, but it can add up to significant savings over the course of a winter.

### Nighttime and away settings for maximum savings

When you're sleeping or away from home, it's a good idea to lower the thermostat to **62°F (16.5°C)**. This can save you up to 10% on heating costs, according to EnergyStar (2023). Each degree of temperature reduction can save you 1% on heating bills, so lowering the thermostat by 10 degrees can save you up to 10% on heating costs.

### Smart thermostat programming tips

To optimize savings without sacrificing comfort, consider the following smart thermostat programming tips:

1. **Start warming 30 minutes before wake-up**: This allows you to wake up to a warm home without wasting energy by heating it for too long.
2. **Lower the thermostat at night**: As mentioned earlier, lowering the thermostat to 62°F (16.5°C) can save you up to 10% on heating costs.
3. **Use a smart thermostat with geofencing**: This feature allows you to set your thermostat to automatically adjust based on your location, so you can save energy when you're away from home.

### Unexpected factors that impact savings

Insulation quality, furnace age, and humidity levels can all impact the effectiveness of temperature adjustments. For example, if your home is poorly insulated, you may not be able to save as much energy by lowering the thermostat. Similarly, if your furnace is old, it may not be as efficient as a newer model, which can impact your energy savings.
