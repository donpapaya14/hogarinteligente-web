## Introduction to Smart Plug Energy Monitoring
With the increasing concern about energy consumption and its impact on the environment, **smart plug energy monitoring** has become a crucial aspect of smart home technology. According to the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov), 2022, smart plugs can help reduce energy waste by monitoring and controlling the power consumption of appliances in real-time. In this article, we will explore how smart plug energy monitoring works, identify the top energy hogs in your home, and provide a step-by-step guide on setting up automated energy-saving schedules.

## How Energy Monitoring Smart Plugs Work
Smart plugs work by using built-in sensors to measure voltage, current, and power consumption in real-time. The data is then transmitted to a cloud server or a mobile app, allowing users to monitor their energy usage remotely. A simple circuit diagram illustrates how the sensors measure the power consumption of an appliance.

| Component | Function |
| --- | --- |
| Voltage Sensor | Measures the voltage supplied to the appliance |
| Current Sensor | Measures the current drawn by the appliance |
| Microcontroller | Processes the data and transmits it to the cloud or mobile app |

According to the [Lawrence Berkeley National Lab](https://www.lbl.gov), 2021, smart plugs can help reduce energy consumption by identifying energy hogs in your home.

## Top 3 Most Surprising Energy Hogs in Your Home
Real-world data shows that some appliances can be unexpected energy hogs, even when they are turned off. The top 3 most surprising energy hogs are:

1. **Gaming Consoles**: Gaming consoles can consume up to 250 watts of power even when they are turned off but still plugged in.
2. **Old Refrigerators**: Old refrigerators can consume up to 200 watts of power, which can increase your energy bill by up to $100 per year.
3. **Televisions**: Televisions can consume up to 100 watts of power even when they are turned off but still plugged in.

## How to Set Up Automated Energy-Saving Schedules
To set up automated energy-saving schedules, follow these steps:

1. **Identify Energy Hogs**: Identify the appliances that consume the most energy in your home.
2. **Set Up Schedules**: Set up schedules to turn off these appliances when they are not in use.
3. **Use If-This-Then-That Rules**: Use if-this-then-that rules to automate your energy-saving schedules. For example, if the usage drops below 5W for 30 minutes, then turn off the appliance.

## Choosing Between Basic vs. Advanced Models
When choosing a smart plug, consider the following features:

* **kWh Tracking**: Tracks the energy consumption of an appliance in kWh.
* **Power Factor Measurement**: Measures the power factor of an appliance, which can help identify energy hogs.
