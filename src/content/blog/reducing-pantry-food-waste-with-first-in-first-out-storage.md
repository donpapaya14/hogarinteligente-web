## Understanding Food Waste Statistics
The average American household throws away **$1,500** worth of food per year, according to the [USDA](https://www.usda.gov) 2020 Report on Food Waste. This staggering figure translates to approximately **30-40%** of the total food supply, with a significant portion of this waste originating from pantry items.

## Implementing a First-In-First-Out System
To reduce pantry food waste, implementing a **first-in-first-out** storage system is crucial. Here's a step-by-step guide:
1. Label each item with its purchase date.
2. Categorize items into groups (e.g., grains, canned goods).
3. Store items in the correct order, with the oldest items at the front.
As recommended by the [Professional Organizer's Association](https://www.napo.net), this system ensures that older items are consumed before they expire.

## Categorizing Pantry Items for Efficient Storage
Categorizing pantry items is vital for preventing cross-contamination and ensuring easy access. The [National Kitchen and Bath Association](https://www.nkba.org) recommends the following categories:
* Grains (e.g., rice, pasta)
* Canned goods (e.g., vegetables, fruits)
* Baking supplies (e.g., flour, sugar)
Store each category separately to maintain a organized and functional pantry.

## Maintaining Your Pantry Organization System
Regularly reviewing and adjusting your pantry organization system is crucial for ensuring continued effectiveness. The [Professional Organizer's Association](https://www.napo.net) suggests:
1. Schedule regular pantry clean-outs (every 3-6 months).
2. Monitor expiration dates and adjust storage accordingly.
3. Adjust categorization and labeling as needed.
